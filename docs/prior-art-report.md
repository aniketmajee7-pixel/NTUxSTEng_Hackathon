# Synthetic Human Agent — Prior Art & Design Report
**NTU × ST Engineering Hackathon 2026 · "Building a Synthetic Human Agent"**

> **Context & authorization.** This is working material for the NUS × ST Engineering 2026 "Synthetic Human Agent" hackathon — a sanctioned cyber-range competition in which each team's agent performs ordinary office and web tasks on an organiser-provided, isolated Windows VM, and is judged on how closely its activity resembles a genuine human user under routine telemetry review (Sysmon, Process Explorer, Process Monitor). There is no real target, no third-party system, and nothing is deployed outside the competition VM. The material below is compiled from published academic papers, open-source user-emulation frameworks, and vendor documentation, for the defensive/competition purpose of building and self-evaluating a realistic synthetic user. Descriptive terms such as "tell," "detectable," "indicator," or "process lineage" refer to how a reviewer tells human activity apart from automated activity for scoring — not to attacking or circumventing any protected system.

Scope: autonomous, human-like office + web activity on a Windows VM for ≥15 min, scored against Sysmon (EID 1,3,5,11,22), Process Explorer, and Process Monitor. Scoring: Process Authenticity 30% · Human Entropy & Timing 30% · Complexity of Action 20% · Resource Efficiency 20%.

## Bottom line
- **Launch through the shell, not from the automation layer.** A `winword.exe` / `chrome.exe` whose Sysmon EID 1 `ParentImage` is `python.exe`, `powershell.exe`, `AutoHotkey.exe`, or a WebDriver exe is the single clearest indicator of automation to a reviewer. Target `explorer.exe → WINWORD.EXE`. Prefer `ShellExecute`/shell activation to launch, then **attach** (pywinauto `connect()`) to drive — never COM-launch Office or Selenium-launch the browser, which is exactly how GHOSTS loses authentic parentage.
- **Timing must be heavy-tailed AND structured, not just high-variance.** Uniform-random gaps get caught (USENIX 2008); the judges' CV > 0.4 hint really means "human-shaped distribution." Sample inter-key intervals from **log-normal** (or log-logistic), page dwell from **Weibull (shape < 1)**, and interleave medium idle gaps between task *blocks* (PHASE: idle-gap camouflage raised humanness 0.31→0.86).
- **Use the real ground-truth numbers.** Desktop typing: mean IKI ≈ **239 ms (SD 112, CV ≈ 0.47)**, hold ≈ **116 ms**, WPM ≈ **52 (SD 20)**, uncorrected error ≈ **1.2%**, backspaces ≈ **6.3%** of keystrokes, rollover ≈ **25%** (Dhakal et al., 136M keystrokes). For typing-as-authorship the human IKI CV runs **~1.0** (Condrey 2026).
- **Model rollover and delayed error-detection explicitly** — both are strong human-vs-automation discriminators in the literature. Sample key *hold* independently of flight time (produces overlap); detect typos a few chars late, then backspace-correct.
- **For the web task, emit a continuous OS-level mouse stream.** The minimal browser-bot detector is just `{mouse_event_rate, teleport_click_ratio}` at 100% recall (TUM 2607.26935). Never click cold; generate intermediate `mousemove`s, vary click hold duration, keep `isTrusted=true`. This is why real input synthesis (SendInput/PyAutoGUI) beats Playwright/CDP DOM dispatch.
- **Don't over-jitter or ship raw Bezier.** Pure geometric-humanizer paths are themselves detectable (~99.6%); layer velocity-scaled, *correlated* tremor. Optimal path randomness is moderate (DMTG α ≈ 0.2–0.3), not extreme.
- **GHOSTS and MITRE Caldera `human` are near-exact prior implementations** — copy their timeline/handler architecture and randomization model, but build the per-keystroke typing realism and shell-launch parentage yourself (both lack it).
- **Let the real apps produce their own side-effects.** A genuine Word save emits `~$lock`, `.tmp`, and AutoRecover writes (EID 11); a real browser emits DNS (EID 22) then TCP (EID 3). Driving the real GUI apps gives you these for free — a bare HTTP client or a fake save looks synthetic.

## (a) Launching apps with a clean process tree — Process Authenticity (30%)

**The rule the judges apply (defender-side ground truth).**
- **Splunk / Syspanda / EpicDetect — Sysmon Office lineage detections** — https://research.splunk.com/removed/b12c89bc-9d06-11eb-a592-acde48001122/ *(Splunk Security Content / Syspanda 2017 / EpicDetect)*. Genuine interactive Office = `WINWORD/EXCEL/OUTLOOK.EXE` parented by `explorer.exe` with **no** script-interpreter children. Automation/macro abuse = Office spawning `cmd/powershell/wscript/cscript/mshta`. Detection is lineage/behavior-based, not signature-based, and unspoofable at the kernel level — so make the *whole tree and side-effects* consistent, don't spoof one field. Prefer UI automation over COM (COM-launched Office is parented by the automating process). Real saves emit companion `~$name.docx` lock files, `.tmp`, and AutoRecover writes under `%AppData%\Microsoft\Word` (EID 11); real browsing emits EID 22 (DNS) → EID 3 (TCP) plus CDN/telemetry side-traffic.
- **T1059 Command & Scripting Interpreter — detection rules** — https://df00tech.com/detections/T1059 *(df00tech, 2026)*. Interpreters flagged as automation drivers to **never** be the parent of Office: `powershell.exe, pwsh.exe, cmd.exe, wscript.exe, cscript.exe, mshta.exe, python.exe, python3.exe, node.exe`, and notably **`AutoHotkey.exe` / `AutoIt3.exe`**. Invert the flagged pairs: you *want* `explorer.exe → winword.exe`. EID 1 fields keyed on: `Image`, `ParentImage`, `CommandLine` — keep command lines free of automation tells (`-EncodedCommand`, hidden-window switches). Same heuristic is encoded across 7 SIEM languages, so it's portable across whatever a judge uses.

**The mechanism to achieve it.**
- **pywinauto docs** — https://pywinauto.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started.html *(Vasily Ryabov, current)*. The crux: `Application(backend='uia').start('notepad.exe')` uses `CreateProcess` → parent becomes `python.exe` (**avoid**). `Application().connect(process=PID | title_re=... | path='outlook.exe', timeout=…)` **attaches to an already-running app** — so launch via the shell, then `connect()` and parentage stays `explorer.exe`. Backends: `win32` for legacy WinForms/MFC; `uia` for WPF/Store/Qt5/browsers/modern Office dialogs (Chrome under uia needs `--force-renderer-accessibility`). Synchronize on dialogs with `dlg.wait('visible'|'ready', timeout=30)` / `wait_not(...)` instead of fixed sleeps (Save-As/Replace/Open popups). `type_keys(text, with_spaces=True)`, `menu_select('Edit->Replace')`, companion `pywinauto.mouse`/`.keyboard`. Caveat: `PsExec -s -i` / `schtasks` can drive GUI in locked sessions but both change ancestry (parent → svchost/taskeng/PsExec).
- **AutoHotkey v2 — Run function** — https://www.autohotkey.com/docs/v2/howto/RunPrograms.htm *(AHK project, current)*. **Shell-verb path is the authenticity mechanism:** `Run '*Verb Target'` and verbs like `Run 'edit ' file` route through **ShellExecute** rather than `CreateProcess` of a named exe — most likely to produce shell/Explorer-mediated parentage. Contrast: plain `Run 'notepad'` is still `CreateProcess` with `AutoHotkey.exe` as parent (and AHK itself is a flagged interpreter — see T1059). PID sync: `Run 'mspaint',,,&pid` then `WinWaitActive 'ahk_pid ' pid`. Two subtle tells to avoid: launched processes inherit AHK's env vars (env-diff analysis), and keep windows **normal/visible** (a hidden window under `explorer.exe` is the masquerade signature).

## (b) Human typing timing models + numbers — Human Entropy (30%) / Office task

**Ground-truth datasets (calibrate against these).**
- **Observations on Typing from 136 Million Keystrokes** — https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/136Mkeystrokes/resources/chi-18-analysis.pdf *(Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson, Oulasvirta — Aalto/Cambridge, CHI 2018; 168k volunteers)*. **The canonical desktop numbers:**
  - WPM mean **51.56 (SD 20.2)**, skew 0.513. Fast cluster >78 WPM, slow <26.
  - Mean IKI **238.7 ms (SD 111.6) → CV ≈ 0.468** (already above the 0.4 floor), skew **1.98**, hard floor ~60 ms. Fast typists ~120 ms (SD ~11); slow >480–900 ms.
  - Keypress **hold 116.24 ms (SD 23.88)**, skew 0.8 — far tighter/less-skewed than IKI, and roughly **speed-independent** (stays 80–150 ms). ⇒ model hold as a *separate tight* distribution, IKI as *heavy-tailed*.
  - Uncorrected error **1.167%** (90% under 2.66%); corrections **2.29 backspace/sentence = 6.3% of keystrokes**; KSPC **1.173**.
  - **Rollover 25% (SD 17%)** — near 0 for slow typists, 40–70% for fast; overlap up to ~100 ms. "Surprisingly prevalent" realism signal.
  - Hand-alternation bigrams ~80 ms *faster* than same-finger → supports digraph-class multipliers. Eight typist clusters → run **multiple distinct profiles**, not one average, for between-session entropy.
- **How do People Type on Mobile Devices?** — https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/typing37k/resources/Mobile_typing_study.pdf *(Palin et al., MobileHCI 2019; 37k volunteers)*. Use only if simulating slower/phone input. Speed **36.17 WPM (SD 13.2)**; uncorrected error **2.34%** with composition **55.6% substitution / 33.3% omission / 11.1% insertion** (mirror these ratios); backspaces **1.89/sentence**; KSPC **1.18**. Posture speeds: two-thumb 50.0 / one-thumb 36.3 / index 33.8 WPM.

**Which distribution to sample from.**
- **On the shape of timings distributions in free-text keystroke dynamics** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8606350/ *(González, Calot, Ierache, Hasperué — Heliyon 2021;7(11):e08413)*. Compared 14 distributions across 3 free-text datasets: **best fit = 3-parameter log-logistic** for *both* hold and flight times (beats log-normal under AIC, best-match count, and Anderson-Darling rejection frequency). **Log-normal is the excellent pragmatic second choice.** Fit/sample flight and hold from **separate** distributions; validate the stream with A-D or KS. Do **not** use Gaussian/normal noise — timings are heavy-tailed and right-skewed. Caveat: keystroke-dynamics identification data, not office-typing volume; the brief's "mixture of two log-normals" claim is **not** supported by this source (treat as unverified).

**The recipe + the exact features a judge computes.**
- **Timing-Forgery Attacks Against Motor-Signal Verification** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17280 *(David Condrey, arXiv:2601.17280, 2026)*. Human IKI **CV (δ) ∈ [0.44, 3.5]**; SBU-corpus mean δ = **0.987 ± 0.188** across 13k sessions. Automated injection sits at δ ∈ [0.05, 0.27]; **equal-error threshold ≈ 0.27**. Aim CV clearly above 0.4, ideally ~1.0. **The 7 features to make human-like:** (1) δ = CV of IKI; (2) mean IKI; (3) IKI variance; (4) pause density ρ = fraction of IKIs > 500 ms; (5) mean burst length β = mean run of IKIs < 150 ms; (6) Shannon entropy of the IKI histogram (50 bins, [0,2000] ms); (7) digraph variability γ = std of consecutive IKI differences. **Winning recipe:** simple **histogram sampling** from an empirical human IKI CDF already achieves ~100% evasion — model typing as alternating fast bursts (IKI < 150 ms) and thinking pauses (IKI > 500 ms) plus occasional backspace-corrections, *not* a constant per-char delay. This reproduces features 4, 5, and the heavy tail at once.

**Ready-to-use implementation parameters.**
- **humaninput** — https://github.com/towa0/humaninput *(towa0, 2024–2026, MIT, Python, numpy-only core)*. Seeded, no-I/O engine: `t = Typist(profile=..., seed=42); t.type("hello")` → byte-identical `EventStream[KeyEvent]`; replay backends are separate (pynput / Playwright-CDP / JSON). **IKI = log-normal**, `touch_typist`: **μ_ms=128, σ=0.42, wpm_target=75**. **Digraph multipliers on the median:** same_key **0.60**, alternating_hand **0.85**, same_hand_diff_finger **1.0** (baseline), same_finger_diff_key **1.60**, to_digit **2.0**; to/from punctuation slower. **Hold sampled independently of IKI ⇒ rollover** ("one of the strongest tells"). **Pace wander = Ornstein-Uhlenbeck in log-space** (raises CV). Bursts = geometric run-length + micro-pauses; cognitive pauses before rare words/digit runs/brackets/sentence starts, shorter after commas. **Error model:** substitution (weighted to physically adjacent keys) / transposition / insertion / omission at independent rates; **detection delayed a few chars** then pause+retype; `arrow_correction_probability` vs backspace-retype; `retype_error_rate_multiplier` + `max_cascade_depth`. Shipped profiles: `touch_typist` 75 WPM, `hunt_and_peck` 30, `fast_coder` 100 (slow on symbols), `mobile_thumbs`, `tired`. **Ships a validation suite** (`humaninput validate`): KS-test on IKIs, digraph-class ordering, WPM tolerance, Fitts R²>0.95, unimodal velocity, seeded reproducibility, round-trip `fit`. `pip install humaninput`.
- **human-keyboard** — https://github.com/luishacm/human-keyboard *(luishacm, 2024, MIT, Python numpy + `keyboard`)*. README defaults: `HumanKeyboard(wpm=80, error_rate=0.1, fatigue_max=2.0, fatigue_increase=0.002, fatigue_recovery=0.001)`. Full tunables: `thinking_delay`, `punctuation/number/space_multiplier`, `shift_delay`, `correction_delay`, `backspace_delay`, per-key-pair `distance_matrix`. **Fatigue model** drifts speed/error over time → rising CoV across a long run (good for the ≥15-min requirement). **PITFALL:** the `keyboard` module injects *global low-level* input (SetWindowsHookEx-style), needs admin, and reads as a background injector in telemetry — pair it with a real focused, shell-launched window.

## (c) Mouse / scroll / reading models + libraries — Web task / Human Entropy

**Reading / dwell-time model.**
- **Understanding Web Browsing Behaviors through Weibull Analysis of Dwell Time** — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1835449.1835513 *(Liu, White, Dumais — Microsoft Research, SIGIR 2010)*. Model per-page dwell as **Weibull, not exponential/Gaussian** (survival-analysis framing: "abandon" = "failure"). **"Negative aging": shape k < 1** (high early-abandon hazard, then long heavy tail) → sample dwell from `Weibull(k≈0.8–0.9)`, giving CV > 1 naturally. **"Screen-and-glean":** a short screening glance precedes any long read → bimodal dwell (many short + occasional long). ~30 s is the classic "engaged read" mode. **Caveat:** exact fitted k/λ not extractable here — pull from the PDF (`susandumais.com/sigir2010-dwelltimemodel.pdf`) before quoting.

**Mouse trajectory — physics baselines (cheap, resource-efficient).**
- **WindMouse** — https://ben.land/post/2021/04/25/windmouse-human-mouse-movement/ *(Ben Land, 2021; algorithm ~2 decades old)*. Cursor = mass under "gravity" pull + stochastic "wind"; curved variable-speed paths, negligible CPU. Defaults **G₀=9, W₀=3, M₀=15 (max step), D₀=12 px**. Two phases: while `dist≥D₀` wind gets smoothed random perturbation; while `dist<D₀` wind only damps and step shrinks to settle. `v += W + G₀·(dest−cur)/dist`, clip to `[M₀/2, M₀]`. Human speed ~5–10 px/ms. Ports in Python/Rust/JS/Java. **The efficient baseline**; DMTG is the high-realism option.
- **humaninput mouse model** (same repo as above). Fitts MT = `a + b·log2(2D/W)` (test asserts R²>0.95); trajectory = one ballistic sub-movement covering **85–95%** on a **minimum-jerk** (Flash & Hogan 1985) profile, then **1–3 corrective sub-movements** (count scales with Fitts difficulty) + occasional overshoot; **correlated value-noise tremor** layered on ("what makes it read as a hand" — *not* per-sample Gaussian jitter).

**Mouse trajectory — production libraries with copyable numbers.**
- **ghost-cursor** — https://github.com/Xetera/ghost-cursor *(Xetera et al., 2019–2026, TS, MIT; Python ports: cursor-humanizer, humancursor-playwright)*. `path(from,to,{useTimestamps:true})` → `{x,y,timestamp}` samples; inter-frame gaps **~48–56 ms** (≈18–20 move events/sec, not a teleport). Bezier control points on **one side** of the straight line; Fitts sets the *number* of points. `move()` defaults: `overshootThreshold=500` (moves <500px don't overshoot), `paddingPercentage=0` (lands random inside element), `randomizeMoveDelay=true`. `click()` defaults: `moveDelay=2000`, `hesitate=0`, `waitForClick=0` → set `hesitate 100–200ms`, `waitForClick 30–100ms`. **Scroll:** `scrollSpeed=100` is **instant = a bot tell; use 40–75**; `scrollDelay=200ms`. `ghost-cursor-patchright` adds **mechanical wheel scrolling**: randomized **5–15 ms** between individual wheel ticks. **Pitfall:** raw Bezier is itself detectable (~99.6%) — add tremor + variable timing.
- **pydoll humanized mouse** — https://github.com/autoscrape-labs/pydoll/blob/main/pydoll/interactions/mouse.py *(autoscrape-labs, 2024–2026, CDP)*. Most directly copyable constants (`MouseTimingConfig`, seconds): overshoot 0.03–0.12 of travel, `overshoot_speed_threshold=200px`, `pre_click_pause 0.05–0.20`, `click_hold 0.05–0.15`, `min_duration=0.08`. **Micro-pause during move:** prob **0.03/frame**, 0.015–0.04 s. Overshoot split: 85% duration to overshoot point, 15% to correct. **Velocity-scaled tremor (key trick):** `speed_factor = max(0.2, 1 − velocity/500)`, `sigma = tremor_amplitude · speed_factor` → **tremor larger when slow, suppressed when fast** (inverse-velocity coupling, not flat jitter). Frame delay always jittered.
- **cloakbrowser-human** — https://github.com/evelaa123/cloakbrowser-human *(evelaa123, 2025–2026, Playwright wrapper)*. **Fills the scroll-spec gap:** `scroll_delta_base=[80,130]px/tick`, `variance=0.2`, two cadence modes `pause_fast=[60,150]ms` / `pause_slow=[150,400]ms`, `accel_steps=[2,3]`/`decel_steps=[2,3]` (ramp, not constant), `overshoot_chance=0.1` + `overshoot_px=[50,150]`, `settle_delay=[300,600]ms`, `target_zone=[0.20,0.80]` (stop when element is 20–80% of viewport, not pinned top), `pre_move_delay=[100,300]ms`. Emits many discrete wheel events (Sysmon "real wheel"). Mouse: `steps=distance/8`, `min_steps=25/max_steps=80`, `burst_size=[3,5]` points + `burst_pause=[8,18]ms`. Typing: base ~90 ms, `pause_chance=0.2`. Click aim keyed to element type ("left third for inputs, center for buttons"). Companion `shy-mouse-playwright`: use **Perlin noise** (not pure random), vary polling **60–144 Hz** with temporal correlation, model fatigue, logarithmic scroll deceleration.
- **DMTG: Entropy-Controlled Diffusion mouse trajectories** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18233 *(Liu, Cui, Ge, Zhan — Michigan / Sichuan, Oct 2024)*. **Controlled randomness:** a human path sits *between* chaos and straight-line; same target → different path each run (exact replay is a tell). **Optimal α ∈ 0.2–0.3** (α≤0.2 too chaotic, α≥0.8 collapses to straight; they sample [0.3,0.8]). Path **entropy ∝ trajectory length** — a cheap proxy to target without a diffusion model. Ease-in/ease-out (direction-dependent acceleration), never constant-velocity. Cut bot-recognition 4.75–9.73%; against a *unified* multi-detector, accuracy only dropped to ~80% (echoes PHASE: holistic scoring is harder to fool). Metrics to optimize toward: JS-divergence, Wasserstein/EMD, cosine similarity vs real distributions. **Over-jittering (GAN/SapiAgent noise) is as detectable as under-jittering.**

## (d) How evaluators distinguish human from automated activity — characteristics to reproduce

- **What Does It Take to Detect an AI Agent? Minimal Feature Sets under Browser Automation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26935 *(Choudhary et al. — TU Munich + Kontext, 2026; recorded Claude driving Chrome via Playwright/MCP)*. **Root cause:** the tell is an *API execution artifact*, not "agent thinking" — `element.click()` fires with **no preceding mouse motion, no wheel deltas, empty pre-click mousemove tracks**, and this survives trajectory manipulation. **7 binary env red flags to avoid:** `no_mouse_events, no_scroll_events, no_keyboard_events, zero_dimensions, webdriver_flag, has_untrusted_events (isTrusted=false), no_micro_jitter`. **Minimal detector = `{mouse_event_rate, teleport_click_ratio}` → 100% agent recall at every evasion level**; backward-elimination pair = `{mouse_event_rate, click_duration_std}`. **Human distribution params to reproduce (their Table 1):** trajectory entropy N(2.8,0.8); movement efficiency N(0.55,0.15) — humans are *inefficient*, bots N(0.92,0.05); click duration N(110,30) ms; event rate N(15,7) Hz; teleport_ratio N(0.05,0.06) (bots N(0.55,0.2)). **The decisive lesson:** all 5 evasion levels (incl. GAN + replayed real human cursor data) still hit 100% detection because the *event-stream structure* is programmatic — they explicitly note OS-level injection (xdotool/PyAutoGUI/hardware/CDP event injection) is *outside their threat model and would defeat it*. **⇒ For our Windows VM, use real input synthesis (SendInput/PyAutoGUI), not DOM automation.** Level-2 baseline params: Bezier paths, click 60–180 ms, inter-action 500–2000 ms; Level-4 added σ=1.5px jitter + Fitts timing.
- **PHASE: Passive Human Activity Simulation Evaluation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13505 *(Lamp, Hiser, Nguyen-Tuong, Davidson — U. Virginia, 2026)*. An ML humanness scorer + SHAP + it re-tunes the MITRE Caldera Human Plugin. Principle to quote: **"WHEN an activity occurs is as important as WHAT activity occurs."** **Winning tactic:** inserting periodic ~1-hour idle periods after each randomized task sequence raised humanness **0.31→0.86** and **0.24→0.71** without changing actions — **for our 15-min run, scale down: interleave randomized medium idle gaps between task *blocks*, not just per-keystroke jitter.** Detector uses **per-minute** aggregated features (1,440 intervals/day): volume/min, burstiness, business-hours concentration, protocol/destination diversity. Accuracy ladder: per-event trees ~50% (fail) → LSTM ~60% → **CNN-BiLSTM-Attention on minute-sequences = 91%**. **Critical pitfall:** temporal camouflage gave only +7 pts in a *fluid* schedule, because the detector then weights intrinsic activity *quality* — so you need **both** varied realistic actions AND human timing. Passive Zeek network logs = exact analogue of Sysmon/ProcMon host telemetry.
- **Measurement and Classification of Humans and Bots in Internet Chat** — https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/sec08/tech/full_papers/gianvecchio/gianvecchio_html/ *(Gianvecchio, Xie, Wu, Wang — USENIX Security 2008)*. Almost certainly the lineage of the "compute entropy/variance of gaps" scoring. Primary feature = inter-message delay; **human IMDs are heavy-tailed/power-law**, bots cluster in narrow bands. Two measures: first-order Entropy (Q=256 bins) and **Corrected Conditional Entropy** (Q=5, captures the entropy *rate*/sequential dependence). Threshold at ~1st percentile of human scores (1% FP). Needs ~100 events for reliable detection — **a 15-min run emits enough to be measured**. **The trap:** "random" bots drawing from a **uniform** distribution were still caught — uniform is unnaturally regular in entropy terms. **Match distribution *shape* (heavy tail), not just a high variance number.** This is the real content behind "CV > 0.4": high *and structured* variability.
- (Detection references also cited under section (a): **T1059 / df00tech** and **Splunk/Syspanda Sysmon Office lineage**.)

## (e) Existing synthetic-user / cyber-range frameworks

- **GHOSTS** *(CMU SEI CERT, 2018–2026, v9; .NET)* — repo https://github.com/cmu-sei/GHOSTS · docs https://cmu-sei.github.io/GHOSTS/ · source https://github.com/cmu-sei/GHOSTS/blob/master/src/Ghosts.Domain/Messages/Timeline.cs. **Near-exact prior implementation.** Three clients: **Windows** (.NET 4.6.2, full Office/browser/RDP — *use this pattern*), **Universal** (.NET 9, 38+ handlers), **Lite** (.NET 8, fake network traffic, **no real apps → would score poorly on Process Authenticity — do NOT use**). Behavior is **declarative JSON**: `Timeline{Id, Status(Run), TimeLineHandlers[]}`; each `TimelineHandler{HandlerType, Initial, UtcTimeOn/Off, HandlerArgs{}, Loop, TimeLineEvents[]}`; each event `{Command, CommandArgs[], DelayBefore, DelayAfter}`. Handlers: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Notepad, BrowserChrome/Firefox, Cmd, Bash, Ssh/Sftp, Rdp, Watcher, Clicks, Print, Wmi, Sharepoint, Blog, Database. **Timing internals (from source — docs omit them):** `DelayAfter/Before` accept a plain int *or* `DelayRandom{Random,Min,Max}` (uniform in range — set wide, e.g. 2000–120000, to widen variance). `Jitter.JitterFactorDelay(base, jf)` = uniform ±jf%, clamped 0–50%; **CRITICAL: at max 50% that's only CV ≈ 0.289 — below the 0.4 threshold, so don't rely on it alone**; use wide `DelayRandom` or a heavy-tailed base. `Jitter.Basic(base)` = `base + rand(−999,1999)` floored at 1 (right-skewed additive). `HandlerArgs`: `execution-probability` (e.g. 90% fire rate), `delay-jitter` (0–50), browser `stickiness`/`stickiness-depth`, `isheadless`+`blockimages/styles/scripts` (set true to cut CPU/RAM for Resource Efficiency), Office `workingset{max, max-age-in-hours}`. Browser commands use **XPath/DOM selectors**, not pixel coords. **Two process-authenticity WARNINGS (the key lesson):** GHOSTS launches Office via **COM/DCOM interop** (+Redemption for Outlook) and browsers via **Selenium WebDriver** → EID 1 parent is the DCOM launcher (svchost/dllhost) or the driver exe, **never explorer.exe** — we must launch differently. And **GHOSTS does NOT simulate per-keystroke typing/typos** (Word.cs does one bulk `TypeText`) — we must build that ourselves. Deploy as non-admin interactive user; `ResourceControl.ManageProcesses=true` reaps runaway processes. v8+ uses WebSocket/SignalR (avoid tight HTTP polling — a CPU/telemetry smell).
- **GHOSTS technical report (CMU/SEI-2018-TR-005)** *(Updyke, Dobson, Podnar et al., Dec 2018; 2026 follow-up "A Decade of GHOSTS in the Machine")* — https://resources.sei.cmu.edu/asset_files/TechnicalReport/2018_005_001_534326.pdf (also at www.sei.cmu.edu). Primary-source justification. **Two-layer architecture:** "Agent" (raw actuation/scheduler, neutral) vs "NPC" (persona/knowledge overlay) — separate scheduler from content in our design. **"Knowledge Horizon"** keeps document/email content coherent (feeds Complexity of Action). Capabilities table: web (browse/enter-text/click), terminal (cmd+powershell), inter-NPC email, Office docs — each with method modes **random / specific / looping** (reuse as behavior vocabulary). Explicit stance: use the **actual installed apps** because "shortcuts to mimic user functionality *will be detected by real players*" — exactly the judges' threat model. **"No activity" is a first-class action** (idle widens the gap distribution). Fidelity target: **"believable, not perfect" / sufficient realism** ("Is that reasonable to see over your shoulder?").
- **MITRE Caldera `human` plugin** — https://github.com/mitre/human *(MITRE, ongoing)*. Closest existing analogue after GHOSTS; the tool PHASE studies. One "human" defined per OS runs continuously in a loop of tasks with **tuned randomization**; drives **real Chrome + native OS apps** (interactive process tree). Deps: Python 3, virtualenv, Chrome — lightweight (Resource Efficiency). Framed explicitly as **"noise to blend into telemetry"** — the framing the Sysmon scoring rewards. Timing knobs live in the wiki/source (read to extract distributions).
- **Realistic simulation of users for IT systems in cyber ranges** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11785 *(Dey, Coste, Totel, Becue — IRISA/Airbus, CAID 2021)*. Endorses **exactly one external agent per VM** (same placement as GHOSTS). **Hybrid method:** deterministic control of *actions* + deep-learning **conditional text generation** for *content* so typed text isn't templated — cheap hackathon substitute: a small local LLM or Markov/template generator seeded from a corpus. Adversary = security-monitoring/honeynet observers. **Caveat:** full paper is French, English abstract only — cite for architecture + hybrid idea, **not** numeric timing.

## Recommended approach for our agent (mapped to the rubric)

**Architecture (from GHOSTS + Dey):** one non-admin interactive process on the VM = a **scheduler/actuation layer** driving a **declarative timeline** (copy GHOSTS's `Timeline → Handler → Event{Command, DelayBefore, DelayAfter}` schema), plus a separate **content layer** (templates/Markov/small local LLM per Dey; GHOSTS "Knowledge Horizon" for coherence). Two handlers minimum: Office and Web.

**Process Authenticity (30%).** Launch every app via **shell activation / ShellExecute** so EID 1 `ParentImage = explorer.exe → WINWORD.EXE / chrome.exe`; then **`pywinauto.connect()`** to drive. Never COM-launch Office, never Selenium/Playwright-launch the browser, never let your `python.exe` be the parent (that's the T1059 red flag GHOSTS falls into). Drive input with **OS-level SendInput/PyAutoGUI** — the TUM paper proves DOM-dispatch is caught 100% regardless of trajectory quality. Never let Office spawn `cmd/powershell/wscript/mshta`. Let the real apps produce their own EID 11 (`~$lock`, `.tmp`, AutoRecover) and EID 22→3 (DNS→TCP) side-effects — don't fake saves or use a bare HTTP client. Keep command lines flag-clean; keep windows visible.

**Human Entropy & Timing (30%).** Sample from **shapes, not just wide uniforms** (USENIX 2008 trap). Typing: log-normal IKI (μ≈128–239 ms), **independent hold ~116 ms → rollover ~25%**, OU pace-wander, bursts of <150 ms runs punctuated by >500 ms cognitive pauses, delayed typo detection + backspace correction (~6.3% of keystrokes, ~1.2% uncorrected). Target typing **CV ~1.0** (Condrey), overall action-gap **CV clearly >0.4** with a genuine heavy tail. Web dwell: **Weibull(k≈0.8–0.9)**, bimodal screen-then-read. Mouse: Fitts-timed, minimum-jerk ballistic + 1–3 corrections, **velocity-scaled correlated tremor** (pydoll), moderate curvature (DMTG α≈0.2–0.3), wheel-scroll with 5–15 ms tick gaps and accel/decel ramps. **Crucially: interleave randomized idle gaps between task blocks** (PHASE) — this is the highest-leverage single move. Ship a **KS/A-D validation harness** (humaninput-style) as judge-facing evidence.

**Complexity of Action (20%).** Cover the GHOSTS handler menu breadth within the two required tasks: Office = open → type with pauses/typos/corrections → format → save named file (real .docx) → occasional PDF export → close; Web = real browser → visit preset sites → irregular scroll → click a link → close. Use GHOSTS's random/specific/looping method modes and non-templated generated content (Dey) so activity *quality* is high — PHASE warns timing alone fails when the schedule is fluid (our 15-min run is).

**Resource Efficiency (20%).** Prefer **WindMouse** (negligible CPU) over a diffusion model as the default path engine; DMTG only if realism budget allows. Event/sleep-driven loop, not tight polling (GHOSTS v8 lesson). Reap runaway processes (`ManageProcesses`). Keep the content generator small/local.
